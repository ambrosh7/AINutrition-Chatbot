Researcher Query 10:24 AM

Can you compare the bioavailability of non-heme iron in spinach versus cooked lentils, and how does ascorbic acid impact absorption kinetics?

chips
Clinical Engine 10:24 AM

Physicochemical Bioavailability Comparison

Both spinach (Spinacia oleracea) and lentils (Lens culinaris) supply non-heme iron predominantly in the insoluble ferric form (Fe³⁺). However, their cellular absorption efficiency diverges significantly due to conflicting antinutritional ligand matrices:

Spinach Matrix Low Net Uptake

High concentration of oxalic acid (~400–900 mg/100g) chelates ferric iron at duodenal pH, forming insoluble ferrous oxalate precipitates that resist brush-border divalent metal transporter 1 (DMT1) uptake.

Cooked Lentils Moderate Net Uptake

Phytic acid (myo-inositol 1,2,3,4,5,6-hexakisphosphate) dominates the inhibitory fraction. Unlike oxalates, endogenous phytates are partially hydrolyzable during hydration, germination, and thermal processing.

conversion_path Ascorbic Acid Reduction Kinetics (Fe³⁺ → Fe²⁺)

Ascorbic acid acts as an electron donor that converts insoluble ferric iron (Fe³⁺) into the soluble ferrous form (Fe²⁺) within the gastric lumen prior to alkalinization in the duodenum. Furthermore, ascorbate forms a low-molecular-weight monomeric chelate that out-competes both oxalate and phytate anions, maintaining solubility at duodenal pH 6.0–7.0.

Researcher Query 10:29 AM

What is the safe refrigerated storage time for homemade chicken bone broth at 38°F (3.3°C)?

chips
Clinical Engine 10:29 AM
Thermal Safety Metric Baseline: USDA Food Safety Standard §3-501.17

At a verified consistent temperature of 38°F (3.3°C), homemade poultry bone broth has a maximum microbiologically safe refrigerated shelf life of 3 to 4 days when sealed in a sterile, airtight glass container. Freezing at ≤0°F (-18°C) extends shelf stability to 4 to 6 months with negligible peptide denaturation.
